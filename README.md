# Posta hut

Learn to recognise the longsword guards of historical European martial arts (HEMA): the *Hut* of the German
tradition and the *posta* of the Italian. Name the guard, or find it — with 3D fencers or the original
manuscript plates.

**Play:** https://crashingtingler.github.io/posta-hut/

Masters: Johannes Liechtenauer, Fiore dei Liberi and Joachim Meyer — plus the seven sword-and-buckler wards of Royal Armouries Ms. I.33, the four rapier guards of Salvator Fabris, the sabre guards and parries of Alfred Hutton and the smallsword guards of Philibert de La Touche.

## Sources and credits

- **Fiore dei Liberi**, *Fior di Battaglia* (c. 1404–10), J. Paul Getty Museum, Ms. Ludwig XV 13 — Getty Open Content (public domain).
- **Joachim Meyer**, *Gründtliche Beschreibung der Kunst des Fechtens* (Strasbourg, 1570) — Leipzig University Library copy, via Wiktenauer (public domain). The logo's crown medallion and the "Meyer 1570" lettering are taken from this book.
- **Royal Armouries Ms. I.33** (*Walpurgis Fechtbuch*, Franconia, c. 1300) — scans by the Royal Armouries; listed as public domain (PD-Art) on Wikimedia Commons. Ward identifications follow the manuscript text (Dieter Bachmann's transcription and translation on Wiktenauer).
- **Salvator Fabris**, *Lo Schermo, overo Scienza d'Arme* (Copenhagen, 1606) — engravings by Nicolaus Andrea of Flensburg, Jan van Halbeeck and Francesco Valesio; scan via Guy Windsor / Wiktenauer (public domain). Guards follow Book 1, Part 1, Chs. 19–32 (translation by A. F. Johnson and Michael Chidester). The 3D fencers wear early-1600s dress; Fabris draws them nude.
- **Alfred Hutton**, *Cold Steel: A Practical Treatise on the Sabre* (London, 1889) — Digital Library of India copy on the Internet Archive (public domain). Guards follow "The Guards" chapter; Plate III (Medium Guard) and Plates IV–VII, Hutton's reproductions of James Miller's 1737 engravings of the Inside, Outside, Hanging and St. George's Guards. Also Plates XXX–XXXI (the Great Stick, Guard and False Guard) and XLI (the Dagger, Guard). Plates IX–XXV: the seventeen parries. The master medallion is the book's frontispiece portrait of Hutton.

III (translation by Wandrille Duchemin): Plates 2–6 and 8–10.
